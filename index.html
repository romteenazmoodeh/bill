About this bill

A bill that would require colleges receiving federal financial assistance to ask for and consider SAT or ACT scores, with an exemption for lower-income applicants.

Sponsor: Representative Romteen AzmoodehBill: H.R. 17Committee: Education and Labor

The bill at a glance

Who it coversFour-year colleges that receive federal financial assistance and consider academic qualifications in admissions.
What it requiresApplicants submit a valid SAT or ACT score (either one, never both), and the college considers it.
What it leaves aloneNo minimum weight for the score. Colleges still decide how much it counts and may weigh grades, essays, and activities.
Who is exemptApplicants at or below the 33rd percentile of household income in their state of residence. They may still submit a score voluntarily, and no one may penalize them for skipping it.
Who enforces itThe Secretary of Education. Compliance becomes a condition of receiving federal financial assistance.
When it startsApplicants for admission for the 2028–2029 academic year and every year after.

Section 1: Short Title and Definitions

This Act may be cited as the “Standardized Testing in College Admissions Act of 2026”.

(a) Covered institution.—The term “covered institution” means a four-year institution of higher education that receives Federal financial assistance and considers academic qualifications in determining undergraduate admission.

(b) Standardized test.—The term “standardized test” means the SAT, administered by the College Board, or the ACT, administered by ACT, Inc.

(c) Secretary.—The term “Secretary” means the Secretary of Education.

Section 2: Standardized Testing Requirement

(a) Requirement.—Except as provided in section 4, each covered institution shall require each applicant for undergraduate admission to submit a valid SAT or ACT score as part of the applicant’s application for admission.

(b) Choice of examination.—A covered institution shall not require an applicant to submit both an SAT and ACT score. An applicant may satisfy the requirement under subsection (a) by submitting a score from either examination.

Section 3: Consideration of Test Scores

(a) Required consideration.—Each covered institution shall consider an applicant’s submitted standardized test score as a component of its undergraduate admissions process.

(b) Institutional discretion.—Nothing in this Act shall be construed to establish a minimum weight that a covered institution must assign to a standardized test score in making an admissions decision.

(c) Additional factors.—Nothing in this Act shall prohibit a covered institution from considering high school grades, coursework, extracurricular activities, essays, recommendations, or any other factor otherwise permitted by law when making an admissions decision.

Section 4: Income-Based Exemption

(a) Exemption.—An applicant whose household income is at or below the 33rd percentile of household income within the applicant’s State of residence shall not be required to submit a standardized test score under section 2.

(b) Determination of thresholds.—The Secretary shall annually determine the household income threshold corresponding to the 33rd percentile for each State using the most recently available data from the Bureau of the Census.

(c) Voluntary submission.—An applicant exempt under subsection (a) may voluntarily submit an SAT or ACT score. If such an applicant submits a score, the covered institution shall consider the score in accordance with section 3.

(d) No penalty for exemption.—A covered institution may not penalize or otherwise disadvantage an applicant solely because the applicant qualifies for the exemption provided under subsection (a) and does not submit a standardized test score.

Section 5: Administration, Enforcement, and Effective Date

(a) Administration.—The Secretary shall administer and enforce the requirements of this Act.

(b) Regulations.—The Secretary may issue such regulations as are necessary to carry out this Act.

(c) Compliance.—Compliance with this Act shall be a condition of a covered institution’s eligibility to receive Federal financial assistance.

(d) Effective date.—The requirements of this Act shall apply to applicants for undergraduate admission for the 2028–2029 academic year and each academic year thereafter.
